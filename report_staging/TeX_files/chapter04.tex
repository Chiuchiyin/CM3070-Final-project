\chapter{Implementation}
\section{Production path}
The implementation is organised under \texttt{Code/advisor}. It contains data preparation, schemas, forecasters, strategies, backtesting, explanation, FinRL adapters and service orchestration. The production UI is \texttt{Code/stock-app/app-express.py}; notebooks are retained as exploration or provenance.

\section{Data and forecasting}
The CSV provider validates canonical long-format data. The Yahoo provider supports cached data and both MultiIndex layouts. Preparation writes deterministic CSV and SHA-256 metadata. Missing tickers are rejected and rate-limited refreshes cannot overwrite valid data. The last-close and moving-average forecasters share a contract. The ESN scales training data, runs a fixed reservoir, discards washout states and fits an output layer. The small saved evaluation gives aggregate ESN MAE 3.04 versus 2.93 for last close, and RMSE 3.67 versus 3.68. ESN directional accuracy is 0.35 versus 0 in this small output.

\section{Strategies and interface}
Equal weight distributes investable capital, buy and hold preserves initial weights, and forecast-ranked allocation selects predicted returns before applying cash floors. The FinRL adapter validates action length, ticker order, observation schema and warm-up. The approved A2C artifact used 20,000 timesteps, seed 42, a 20-period lookback, five tickers (AAPL, JNJ, JPM, MSFT and PG), cash actions and net-log-return reward.

The backtester charges 10 basis points transaction cost and 5 basis points slippage. The deterministic explanation reports risk profile, forecasts, weights, cash, date and historical metrics. Optional Qwen output passes through a restricted facts tool and numeric-claim validation. The app provides Market Data, Summary, Portfolio, Forecast and Evaluation views, a Plotly candlestick/SMA chart and recoverable error states. The project environment passed 51 offline tests.
\section{From preliminary prototype to final implementation}
The preliminary report described three modules tested mostly in isolation: an ESN trained on AAPL closing prices, an A2C FinRL environment and a Qwen3/Smolagents dialogue prototype. The final implementation preserves these ideas but changes their engineering status. The ESN is placed behind a forecaster contract with training-only scaling and walk-forward evaluation. The A2C path becomes a separately labelled legacy artifact with explicit dates, action conventions, cash handling and costs. The language layer is connected through an explanation context and a read-only facts tool.

The migration addresses weaknesses identified in the draft. The earlier RC experiment reported negative $R^2$ during rapid price growth; the final report retains this warning by comparing ESN with last close and avoiding universal claims. The earlier RL prototype omitted transaction costs and risk constraints; the final backtester charges costs and reports turnover and drawdown. The earlier language demo could confuse tickers; the final validator rejects unsupported numeric claims and falls back when output is ungrounded.

\section{Forecast algorithm}
The ESN receives a scaled sequence. A fixed recurrent reservoir transforms each input into a high-dimensional state, a washout interval removes unstable initial states, and regularised least squares fits the output layer. During walk-forward evaluation each prediction uses only the history available on that date. The evaluator writes predictions, metrics, seeds and provenance so results are reproducible rather than dependent on a notebook state.

\section{FinRL environment and quality assurance}
The A2C environment uses rolling returns and current weights as observations and adds a cash action. The adapter validates ticker order, action length, observation schema and warm-up. Metadata stores the dataset digest, partitions, seed, lookback, reward and cost assumptions. Training is offline; the Shiny request path performs inference or uses the production baseline and never starts a training job.

Tests are organised by risk: invalid data and ticker correction; training-only scaling and chronology; non-negative weights and cash sums; cost and metric calculations; policy metadata; explanation grounding; and Shiny views, disclosures and recoverable states.
